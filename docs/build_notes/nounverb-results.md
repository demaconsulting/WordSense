# Noun/Verb Lemmas (Phase 5)

Phase 5 adds common English words that are both a noun and a verb (`access`, `chip`, `drill`, ...) to the table with
two senses each, `noun` and `verb`, so that a caller can ask "noun or verb?" for them (`ClassifyTagAsync`,
`IsTagAsync`). Every sentence is real text (Tatoeba, Project Gutenberg, UD EWT, UD CTeTex and a few user-supplied
sentences); the project wrote no sentence. A lemma enters the table only when it passes the quality gate described
below.

## Method

1. **Candidates.** A pilot harvest and a targeted harvest (18,164 rows, 1,378 lemmas) were merged and de-duplicated
   (17,660 rows after removing 498 rows of the 24 lemmas that were already trained). A top-up harvest took tagger
   hits (spaCy `en_core_web_sm`) for 277 lemmas (round 1, 8,974 rows) and for 184 more lemmas (round 2, 6,464 rows),
   at most five rows of one source group per sense, targeting 26 rows per sense.
2. **Judging.** Each top-up row was judged blind by one judge against a noun/verb menu (`noun`, `verb` or `none`, with
   a confidence). A row is accepted when the judge agrees with the tagger and the confidence is `H` or `M`; UD EWT
   rows carry gold annotation and are accepted without judging. Disagreements are dropped (no second judge):

   | Round | Rows | Judged | Accepted | Rate | Gold (UD) | Dropped |
   | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
   | 1 | 8,974 | 8,839 | 8,422 | 95.28 % | 135 | 417 |
   | 2 | 6,464 | 6,360 | 5,942 | 93.43 % | 104 | 418 |

   All judge output files were complete and well formed in both rounds (no chunk had to be judged again).
3. **Import.** `nounverb-import --corpus corpus --name nounverb-1` read the merged pool and both accepted rounds
   (32,263 rows with provenance), with the exclusion list `tools/real-text/nounverb-exclusions.tsv`, the irregular
   forms `tools/real-text/nounverb-forms.tsv`, at least 14 anchors and 8 holdout rows per sense and at most 40 and 12.
4. **Gate.** `nounverb-gate --corpus corpus --model model --apply true` builds the table in memory, evaluates every
   noun/verb lemma on its holdout rows and moves failing lemmas out of the corpus (see "Quality Gate").
5. **Rebuild.** The table was rebuilt from the gated corpus; `validate-provenance` and `validate-pos` pass.

### Forms and Collisions

The importer generated 1,793 forms for the 426 lemmas (`-s`/`-es`/`-ies`, `-ed`, `-ing` with e-drop, doubling and
`c` to `ck`, plus the irregular forms of the forms file) into `corpus/forms/speech.tsv`, the only forms file the build
reads. A form that is also another lemma, a form of another lemma or an inflection of a lemma that is already trained
is left out and logged; every sentence whose target is a collision form is dropped, and the importer fails on any form
that two lemmas would claim. This import found one such form: `bases` of `base`, because `bases` is a trained lemma
of its own. Known collisions such as `bit`, `ground`, `felt`, `fell`, `rose`, `found`, `left` and `saw` stay excluded
through the exclusion list. The 24 heteronym lemmas that were trained before are untouched: no row, sense or form of
them changed. `fall` stays untrained, so the README sample output did not change in its lemmas.

## Quality Gate

The gate is part of the builder (`nounverb-gate`); it runs the same holdout evaluation as `build`, per lemma:

| Check | Threshold |
| --- | ---: |
| Holdout best-sense accuracy of the lemma | at least 90 % |
| Answered accuracy (after abstaining) of each sense | at least 80 % |
| Coverage (share of holdout rows answered) | at least 70 % |

A lemma that fails a check is removed from `anchors`, `holdout`, `provenance` and `senses` of `nounverb-1` and from
`forms/speech.tsv`, and recorded in `corpus/audit/nounverb-dropped.tsv` (`lemma`, reason, anchors, holdout, best-sense
accuracy, answered accuracy per sense, coverage). A later `nounverb-import` skips the lemmas of that file, so a
re-import gives the same corpus. The gate is run again after every drop until no lemma fails (here a second run
passed all 329).

| Gate outcome | Lemmas |
| --- | ---: |
| Evaluated | 426 |
| Passed (trained) | 329 |
| Dropped | 97 |
| of which below the best-sense threshold | 75 (45 on that check alone) |
| of which a sense below the answered-accuracy threshold | 17 |
| of which below the coverage threshold | 38 (21 on that check alone) |

Most lemmas have exactly 8 holdout rows per sense (the holdout is taken from whole source groups so that it shares no
source with the anchors), so one holdout row is worth 6 points of lemma accuracy: 14 of 16 correct (87.5 %) fails and
15 of 16 (93.8 %) passes. The gate is therefore strict and noisy for single lemmas; it favors a smaller table whose
lemmas were each right on 15 of 16 held-out rows over a larger one. The figures after the gate are optimistic for the
same reason (the holdout that selects a lemma also scores it); the real-text evaluation and the independent audit
sample are the unbiased checks.

The 329 new lemmas have 11,566 anchors (35.2 per lemma) and 5,296 holdout rows. Their answered accuracy is 99.34 %
at 91.61 % coverage; noun senses reach 99.06 % and verb senses 97.48 % best-sense accuracy (macro average over
lemmas). For these lemmas the noun-versus-verb tag accuracy equals the best-sense accuracy, because each has exactly
one noun and one verb sense; the table lists both. The real-text evaluation of the existing 689-row set is unchanged
(92.60 % best sense, 96.57 % answered accuracy, 84.62 % answered), because the new lemmas are not in that set. No
real-text rows were set aside for the new lemmas; the independent audit sample of 400 final rows (200 per sense, anchors
and holdout, one or two rows per lemma) is prepared for a later blind judge.

Size and time: the embedded table grows from 770,840 to 1,306,779 bytes (1.31 MB, well under the 5 MB guard). The
full `build` of the final corpus takes 63 seconds on the development machine (including the ONNX encoding of every
anchor and holdout sentence); `learning-curve` takes about 3.5 minutes.

Aggregate for the Simplified Technical English use case: 107 of the 329 trained lemmas are in the maintainer's local
STE-100 working list (counted locally; the list itself is not part of the repository).
