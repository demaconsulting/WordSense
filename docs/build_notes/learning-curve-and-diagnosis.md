# Learning Curve and Error Diagnosis

All figures on this page come from one run of the builder commands `learning-curve`, `error-diagnosis` and
`weak-lemmas` on the committed corpus; the page is generated by `weak-lemmas --report` and the underlying files
are in `corpus/audit/analysis/`:

```text
dotnet run --project tools/WordSense.Builder -c Release -- learning-curve --corpus corpus --model model --out corpus/audit/analysis
dotnet run --project tools/WordSense.Builder -c Release -- error-diagnosis --corpus corpus --model model --out corpus/audit/analysis
dotnet run --project tools/WordSense.Builder -c Release -- weak-lemmas --analysis corpus/audit/analysis --report docs/build_notes/learning-curve-and-diagnosis.md
```

## Method

Three evaluation sets are used: `holdout` (every holdout row, project-written and real), `real` (the holdout rows
whose provenance origin is `real`; a subset of `holdout`) and `real-eval` (the separate real-text evaluation
sentences, never part of the holdout). Sentences are encoded once with the real model and centered with the mean of
all anchors, as in the table build.

- **Learning curve**: for each lemma the classifier is trained on 2, 4, 7, 10, 14 randomly chosen anchors per sense
  (a sense with fewer anchors contributes all of them) and on all anchors, with the settings of the table build. Each
  count except `all` uses several random subsets whose seeds come from a stable FNV-1a hash of the lemma, the count and
the
  subset index, so a run is repeatable. A row is answered when its score margin reaches the lemma's abstain margin
chosen
  for the full anchor set. The plateau estimate takes the slope of accuracy per added anchor per sense between the last
  point and the latest earlier point at least 3 anchors per sense away, and extrapolates it linearly to twice the
anchors,
  limited to the remaining error; a curve is plateaued when the slope is below 0.002 per anchor (an estimate, not a
  guarantee).
- **Error diagnosis**: for each holdout or real-evaluation row that is misclassified or abstained, `s_same` and
  `s_other` are the
  cosine similarities to the nearest anchor of the labeled sense and of any other sense of the lemma. The class is
  `coverage-gap` when both are below 0.77; `overlap` when both reach 0.77 and `s_other` is at least
  `s_same` minus 0.05; otherwise `ambiguous`, a label-noise suspect (the labeled sense's nearest anchor is clearly
closer yet the
  classifier disagrees, or the sentence is far from its own label but near another).
- **Weak lemmas**: a lemma is weak when its holdout accuracy is below 95 % or, with at least 3 real-text rows, its
  real-text accuracy is below 90 %. The score is the mean of the holdout and real-text error rates (the holdout error
rate
  alone without real-text rows). The recommendation follows the dominant error class (ties: coverage gap, overlap,
  ambiguous): `more-sentences` for coverage gaps (expected gain: the smaller of the gap share of the rows and the
  learning-curve gain at twice the anchors), `stronger-encoder` for overlaps on a plateaued curve and `consumer-rule`
for
  overlaps on a rising curve (expected gain: the overlap share of the rows, an upper bound), and `fix-labels` for
ambiguous
  rows (expected gain: the ambiguous share of the rows, an upper bound).

## Conclusions

- On `holdout` the macro accuracy rises from 85.46 % at 2 anchors per sense to 97.12 % with all anchors (mean 16.1307
  per sense); between 10.0000 and 16.1307 anchors per sense the slope is 0.0026 per anchor, so doubling the anchors is
  expected to add about 2.88 %. 443 of 716 lemma curves are plateaued.
- On `real` the macro accuracy rises from 85.72 % at 2 anchors per sense to 97.49 % with all anchors (mean 17.7459 per
  sense); between 13.9988 and 17.7459 anchors per sense the slope is 0.0022 per anchor, so doubling the anchors is
  expected to add about 2.51 %. 265 of 409 lemma curves are plateaued.
- On `real-eval` the macro accuracy rises from 80.52 % at 2 anchors per sense to 93.54 % with all anchors (mean 15.6111
  per sense); between 10.0000 and 15.6111 anchors per sense the slope is 0.0056 per anchor, so doubling the anchors is
  expected to add about 6.46 %. 22 of 39 lemma curves are plateaued.
- Of 1457 diagnosed errors (holdout and real-eval) 224 are coverage gaps (15.37 %), 942 are overlaps (64.65 %) and 291
  are ambiguous (19.97 %).
- 233 lemmas are weak: 47 `more-sentences`, 96 `stronger-encoder`, 71 `consumer-rule` and 19 `fix-labels`. The expected
  gains are estimates: gap gains come from the learning-curve extrapolation, the others are upper bounds.

### Plateau

The macro accuracy on the holdout rises from 85.46 % at 2 anchors per sense to 97.12 % with all anchors (mean 16.13).
Most of the gain is in by 7 anchors (94.07 %); from 10 to all anchors the macro gain is 1.6 points, and the gain at
twice the anchors is estimated at 2.88 %, down from 3.69 % before. 443 of 716 curves are plateaued (62 %, before
58 %). Split by group, with the same anchors per sense:

| Group | Lemmas | 2 | 4 | 7 | 10 | 14 | all | Plateaued |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| New noun/verb lemmas | 329 | 86.83 % | 91.89 % | 95.09 % | 96.48 % | 97.67 % | 98.27 % | 223 (68 %) |
| Older lemmas | 387 | 84.29 % | 90.12 % | 93.20 % | 94.70 % | 95.87 % | 96.14 % | 220 (57 %) |

The noun/verb lemmas start higher and are flatter: each has exactly two senses, so a wrong answer is one of two
choices, and each has about 17.6 anchors per sense. The extra anchors pay little for these lemmas: the 14-to-all step
adds 0.6 points.

### Coverage Gap Against Overlap

| Group | Holdout rows | Errors (wrong or abstained) | Coverage gap | Overlap | Ambiguous |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| New noun/verb lemmas | 5,296 | 477 | 104 (21.8 %) | 248 (52.0 %) | 125 (26.2 %) |
| Older lemmas | 7,493 | 854 | 102 (11.9 %) | 601 (70.4 %) | 151 (17.7 %) |

For the noun/verb lemmas overlap is still the largest class but smaller than for the older lemmas, and the coverage
gap and ambiguous shares are larger: a noun and a verb of one word often appear in similar sentences (`pass`,
`point`, `list`), but the lemma has many, quite different noun uses and verb uses, so some holdout rows lie far from
every anchor. The ambiguous share (26 %) is also where a few rows may be mislabeled; the blind audit in
`nounverb-results.md` found 3 label disagreements in 400 rows (0.75 %), so most ambiguous rows are hard sentences, not
wrong labels.
